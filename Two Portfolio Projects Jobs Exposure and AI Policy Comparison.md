# Two Portfolio Projects: Jobs Exposure and AI Policy Comparison

Project plans for the 3-week job push. The two projects feed each other: the jobs project shows who is exposed to LLM automation, and the policy project checks whether national AI plans address it.

## Project 1: Jobs exposure

**Question:** Which common Nigerian remote and freelance jobs are most exposed to LLM automation, and how well do existing exposure studies fit them?

### Steps

1. **Pick 10 to 12 job categories** that Nigerians actually do remotely, such as customer support, data annotation, content writing, virtual assistance, bookkeeping, transcription, translation, graphic design, tutoring, and entry-level coding.
2. **Break each into 5 to 8 tasks.** Use O*NET task lists as a starting point, but adjust them for Nigerian work, since O*NET is built on US jobs.
3. **Score every task with a rubric you publish:** 0 = not exposed, 1 = partly exposed, 2 = largely exposed. Define each level in one sentence so readers can disagree with you.
4. **Test a sample.** Run 2 or 3 tasks per category through two or three models and rate the output quality. Your own scores will be better evidence than reading studies alone.
5. **Validate with real workers.** Ask 10 to 15 people in your communities who do these jobs to rate the same tasks, then compare with your scores. A mismatch is a finding, not a failure.
6. **Compare with published exposure research.** The ILO's generative AI exposure work and the GPTs are GPTs paper are good starting points. Note where your results differ and why, for example payment barriers, connectivity, language, and informal work.

### Output

A ranked table, two charts, and a 3 to 4 page brief with a limitations section and a short how-I-verified-this note.

Do not present the scores as a forecast of job losses. They show which tasks are technically exposed, which is a different claim.

### Checklist

- [ ] Job categories chosen
- [ ] Task lists written
- [ ] Rubric written and published
- [ ] Model test run and rated
- [ ] 10 to 15 worker ratings collected
- [ ] Compared with published research
- [ ] Charts and brief written
- [ ] Verification note and limitations added
- [ ] Published on Medium and olaoluwa.work

## Project 2: Policy comparison

**Question:** What do AI policies in Nigeria and a few other African countries commit to, and where are they vague?

### Starting facts

Nigeria's National AI Strategy was published in September 2025 and covers 2025 to 2029. According to the OECD.AI Policy Navigator and Digital Policy Alert, it is non-binding. As of mid-2026, Nigeria has no standalone AI law, though several bills in the National Assembly would add rules on AI governance and providers. The strategy also aims to equip 70% of the youth workforce with foundational AI skills. This status can change, so check the current position before you publish.

### Steps

1. **Choose 3 to 4 countries** that have a published strategy or policy, with Nigeria as the anchor. Kenya, Egypt, Rwanda, and South Africa are candidates, and the African Union's continental strategy is worth including. Confirm each document exists and read the original, not news coverage.
2. **Build a rubric of about 10 criteria,** each scored 0, 1, or 2:
   - binding or non-binding
   - named responsible body
   - funding or budget stated
   - measurable targets with deadlines
   - jobs and workforce provisions
   - risk and safety provisions
   - data protection links
   - enforcement or penalties
   - local language or context provisions
   - monitoring and evaluation mechanism
3. **Cite the page** for every score. That is what makes it checkable.
4. **List the vague commitments** in each document, quoting only short phrases and citing page numbers.
5. **Check the AI safety angle:** do any of these documents mention frontier model risks, model evaluation, or incident reporting? Report the answer whichever way it goes. An honest finding that none of them address this is still useful.

### Output

A comparison matrix plus a 2 to 3 page write-up with five observations.

### Checklist

- [ ] Countries chosen and documents located
- [ ] Rubric written
- [ ] Every score has a page citation
- [ ] Vague commitments listed
- [ ] AI safety angle checked
- [ ] Matrix and write-up done
- [ ] Published on Medium and olaoluwa.work

## Connecting the two

In the policy brief, look at what Nigeria's strategy says about jobs, such as its goal of AI skills for most of the youth workforce, and compare it with what your exposure analysis finds. That link turns two projects into one body of work.

This fits the 3-week plan: jobs exposure in Week 1, policy comparison in Week 2. Both are tight, so decide a minimum version up front: 8 job categories and 3 countries if time runs short.
