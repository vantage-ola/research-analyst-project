# Nigeria's strategy vs Project 1 findings

All page numbers are PDF pages of the National AI Strategy (Sept 2025). Project 1 refers to `job-exposure/output/brief.md`.

| Nigeria's strategy says | Page | Project 1 found | The gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| "reduce unemployment by five percentage points by equipping at least 70%" of 16 to 35 year olds with AI skills | p35 | AI already does much of the entry-level text, admin and code work young Nigerians use to start remote careers | The target has no deadline, and the skills it names aren't tied to the jobs AI is changing. |
| A "horizon scan/foresight study" to "identify industries and job roles most susceptible to automation" | p45 | A small version of that study, on 12 remote jobs and 84 tasks | The study has no owner, date or budget anywhere in the 81 pages. |
| Worker protection: "retraining programmes, tailored unemployment benefits" | p45 | Remote and freelance workers are paid per task by foreign clients, outside formal employment | The protections are listed as "examples" for future legislative reform. Nothing says how they would reach freelancers. |
| Local context: "Replicating Nigeria's social context and cultural diversity with AI tools" | p38 | AI's clearest failures were in Yoruba and Nigerian facts | The strategy never names Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo, Pidgin or any Nigerian language. "Language" appears only in "Large Language Model". |
| Job displacement is among the "top ethical and social concerns" | p41 | Data annotation, a fast-growing remote job, is almost invisible to the big studies | The strategy doesn't mention data annotation, freelancing or remote work. |

Other documents for comparison:
- Egypt commits to "fine-tuning Large Language Models for Arabic language use" (p27).
- The AU calls for investment in "local and indigenous African languages" (p55).
- Kenya mentions "a maternal health chatbot in local dialects" (p83).

Outside the document: in August 2026 the minister publicly pushed for local-language AI ([Techeconomy](https://www.techeconomy.ng/tijani-fg-pushes-local-language-ai-expands-fibre-infrastructure)). That's a statement, not part of the strategy, and the brief should say so.
